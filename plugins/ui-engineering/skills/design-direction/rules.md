# Design direction rules

Direction must be task-appropriate and explicitly tied to the Design Brief and lock. Respect explicit visual requests; when they conflict with readability, accessibility, or locked semantics, preserve the intent through a safer realization.

Commit instead of hedging: a direction is a set of decisions (thesis, signature moves, surface model, type pairing, motion character), not a list of adjectives. Under Elevate, keep brand hues, logo, navigation, routes, content and data; change composition, hierarchy, type scale, surfaces and motion.

Avoid generic defaults (catalogued with replacements in the [default banlist](../../knowledge/visual-language/anti-slop/default-banlist.md))—unmotivated purple/blue gradients, ubiquitous glass, arbitrary shadows, floating blobs, huge hero text without purpose, card-everything layouts, pill-everything controls, and motion without feedback value. Intentional style is allowed when its purpose and constraints are recorded.
